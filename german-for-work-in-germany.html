German for jobs in Germany: which level do employers expect?

There is no single rule. The level depends on the job, the employer and – for regulated professions such as nursing – the recognition office of the federal state. This table shows typical expectations, not legal minimums.

Job typeTypical levelWhy
Warehouse, logistics, production helperA2–B1Safety instructions, shift plans, talking to the team
Retail, hotel, restaurantB1, often B2Daily contact with customers
Ausbildung (vocational training)B1–B2Vocational school lessons and exams are in German
Nursing (Pflege)B1 or B2, depending on the federal stateNeeded for recognition; patients and documentation
DoctorsUsually B2 plus a medical language exam at C1Set by the state medical authorities
IT, engineering, researchEnglish often enough to start; German A2–B1 helps a lotTeam language may be English, but daily life is in German
Opportunity Card (Chancenkarte)Language skills count towards the pointsCheck make-it-in-germany.com for the current rules

For official information on visas, recognition and the Opportunity Card, see make-it-in-germany.com, the German government portal for skilled workers. For certificates, see goethe.de and telc.net.

Your job interview in German

Interviews (Vorstellungsgespräche) in Germany are usually formal: use Sie, be on time and keep your answers short and concrete. Here are typical questions with sample answers you can adapt.

„Erzählen Sie bitte etwas über sich.“

„Ich heiße Sara Khan, bin 27 Jahre alt und komme aus Lahore. Ich habe Krankenpflege studiert und drei Jahre in einem Krankenhaus gearbeitet.“
“My name is Sara Khan, I'm 27 and I come from Lahore. I studied nursing and worked in a hospital for three years.”

„Warum möchten Sie bei uns arbeiten?“

„Ihr Haus hat einen sehr guten Ruf, und ich möchte mich in der Pflege weiterentwickeln.“
“Your hospital has a very good reputation, and I want to develop further in nursing.”

„Was sind Ihre Stärken?“

„Ich bin zuverlässig, arbeite gern im Team und lerne schnell.“
“I'm reliable, I like working in a team and I learn quickly.”

„Wie gut sprechen Sie Deutsch?“

„Ich habe das B1-Zertifikat und lerne jeden Tag weiter, weil ich bald B2 erreichen möchte.“
“I have the B1 certificate and keep learning every day, because I want to reach B2 soon.”

„Wann können Sie anfangen?“

„Sobald mein Visum da ist, kann ich sofort anfangen.“
“As soon as I have my visa, I can start right away.”

„Haben Sie noch Fragen an uns?“

„Ja, gern. Wie sieht die Einarbeitung in den ersten Wochen aus?“
“Yes, please. What does the training look like in the first few weeks?”

Tip: always have one question ready for the end. Saying “Nein, keine Fragen” can sound uninterested.

Living in Germany: the first steps

A new job comes with paperwork. Knowing the words saves you a lot of stress in your first month:

  • Anmeldung: register your address at the Bürgeramt, usually within two weeks of moving in. You need the confirmation from your landlord (Wohnungsgeberbestätigung).
  • Health insurance (Krankenversicherung): compulsory in Germany. As an employee you choose a health insurance fund, and your employer handles the contributions through your payslip.
  • Tax ID (Steuer-Identifikationsnummer): usually sent to you by post after your Anmeldung. Your employer needs it for your salary.
  • Bank account (Girokonto): you need one to receive your salary and pay rent.

FAQ: German for work in Germany

Can I get a job in Germany without speaking German?

In some fields, such as IT or research at international companies, English can be enough to start. For most jobs – and for Ausbildung and nursing – German is expected. Even where it is not required, German makes daily work, the job search and life outside work much easier.

Which German level do I need for the Opportunity Card?

The Opportunity Card (Chancenkarte) uses a points system in which language skills play a role, and better German can earn more points. The exact requirements can change, so check make-it-in-germany.com for the current rules before you apply.

Is B1 enough to work in Germany?

B1 is a common minimum for many jobs and for Ausbildung, because you can handle everyday situations and simple workplace conversations. Jobs with a lot of customer contact, nursing and most office roles often expect B2. Your employer or the recognition office decides.

Does Deutsch30 issue a German certificate for my job application?
