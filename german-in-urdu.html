Why Urdu speakers have an advantage in German

German looks far away from Urdu, but your mother tongue already gives you some useful habits:

  • Nouns have gender. In Urdu you already say achhi kitaab but achha kamra. German also has gender – it just has three (der, die, das) instead of two.
  • Formal and informal “you”. German Sie is your aap, and du is your tum/tu. Use Sie with strangers, at offices and at work.
    Aap kaise hain? = Wie geht es Ihnen?
    Tum kaise ho? = Wie geht es dir?
  • Verb at the end. In German side clauses (after weil, dass, wenn) the verb goes to the end – just like in Urdu.
    …, weil ich Deutsch lerne. = …kyunke main German seekh raha hoon.
  • Sounds you already know. The “ch” in Buch is your خ, and “sch” is your ش. Many English speakers struggle with these – you won't.

One difference to remember: in a normal German main clause the verb comes second, not last. Ich lerne heute Deutsch. (I am learning German today.) Main aaj German seekh raha hoon – Urdu puts the verb at the end, German does not.

der, die, das – explained for Urdu speakers

Urdu has two genders, German has three: der (masculine), die (feminine) and das (neuter). The tricky part is that German gender often does not match Urdu, so never translate the gender – learn it with the word.

GermanEnglishGerman genderاردوUrdu gender
das Buchbookneuterکتابfeminine
der Tischtablemasculineمیزfeminine
die Türdoorfeminineدروازہmasculine
die Sonnesunfeminineسورجmasculine
der Mondmoonmasculineچاندmasculine
das Wasserwaterneuterپانیmasculine
das Mädchengirlneuter (because of -chen)لڑکیfeminine

Rules that help

  • Words ending in -ung, -heit, -keit, -schaft, -ion are die: die Zeitung, die Freiheit, die Information.
  • Words ending in -chen or -lein are das: das Mädchen, das Brötchen.
  • Days, months and seasons are der: der Montag, der Mai, der Winter.
  • In the plural, every noun takes die: die Bücher, die Tische, die Türen.

In Urdu, gender shows in the verb and adjective: larka achha hai, larki achhi hai. In German it shows in the article and the adjective ending: ein guter Tisch, eine gute Tür, ein gutes Buch. Practise this with the Der-Die-Das Dash game and the grammar A–Z.

FAQ: learning German in Urdu

Is the Deutsch30 app in Urdu?

No – to be honest, the app's interface and explanations are in English. This page gives you Urdu help for the basics: sounds, first sentences, numbers and der/die/das. If you can read simple English, you can follow the course, and all German audio and examples are the same for everyone.

Is it really free?

Yes. Deutsch30 is 100% free, with no registration and no payment. Your progress is saved in your own browser.

Can I prepare for Goethe A1 with Deutsch30?

Yes. The exam section prepares you for Goethe and telc exams from A1 to B1 with a roadmap, practice tests in the official task formats and timed mock exams. Deutsch30 is independent and not affiliated with Goethe-Institut or telc, and it does not issue certificates. You register for the real exam with the Goethe-Institut or a licensed exam centre – check their official website for current dates and fees.

How long does it take to learn German up to A1?

It depends on your time and practice. The 30-day course gives you a solid beginner base if you study every day, and the exam roadmap plans A1 to B1 over 12 weeks. Short, daily practice works better than one long session per week.

Does it work on my mobile phone?

Yes. Deutsch30 runs in the browser on Android phones, iPhones and computers, with nothing to install. For speaking practice you need a microphone and a browser that supports speech recognition, for example Chrome.
