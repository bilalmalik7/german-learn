German numbers 1 to 100 – and everything after

Here are the German numbers 1 to 100 in clear tables, plus the one rule that confuses every beginner (einundzwanzig), how Germans say prices, phone numbers and years, ordinal numbers for dates, and a quick look at clock times.

The first twenty numbers are the ones you must learn by heart. Everything above 20 is built from them. Watch the spelling of sechzehn (no s) and siebzehn (no -en) – they lose a few letters.

Tip: elf (11) and zwölf (12) are special words, like English “eleven” and “twelve”. From 13 to 19 you simply put the unit before zehn: drei + zehn = dreizehn.

21 to 29: the “unit before ten” rule

This is the big one. In German you say the unit first, then und (and), then the ten – all written as one word. So 21 is literally “one-and-twenty”. Note that eins becomes ein here.

The same pattern works for every number up to 99: 34 = vierunddreißig, 58 = achtundfünfzig, 76 = sechsundsiebzig, 99 = neunundneunzig. When you hear a number, write the second digit first – it becomes automatic after a few days.

Hundreds, thousands and a million

Bigger numbers are put together like building blocks, from big to small – only the last two digits keep the “unit before ten” order. Everything below one million is written as one word. Million and Milliarde are separate nouns with a plural.

Careful: German eine Milliarde is the English billion, and German eine Billion is a million million (an English trillion). German also writes 1.000 with a dot (or a space) and uses a comma for decimals: 2,5 = “zwei Komma fünf”.

Prices: drei Euro fünfzig

For prices you say the euros, then the word Euro, then the cents – usually without the word Cent. The euro symbol normally comes after the number.

Useful sentences: Was kostet das? (How much is that?) – Das kostet drei Euro fünfzig. At the till you will often hear Das macht zwölf Euro, bitte.

Phone numbers and “zwo”

Germans read phone numbers either digit by digit or in pairs. Because zwei and drei can sound alike on the phone, many people say zwo for 2. You will also hear zwo in announcements – it is correct, just extra clear.

Example: 0176 23 45 89 → “null eins sieben sechs – dreiundzwanzig – fünfundvierzig – neunundachtzig”, or digit by digit: “null eins sieben sechs, zwo drei, vier fünf, acht neun”.

Ask for a number with Wie ist Ihre Telefonnummer? (formal) or Wie ist deine Handynummer? (informal).

Ordinal numbers: erste, zweite, dritte

Ordinals (first, second, third…) are used for dates and floors. From 1 to 19 you add -te, from 20 on you add -ste. In writing, a dot after the number makes it an ordinal: der 1. Mai is read “der erste Mai”.

Ordinals take adjective endings. For dates you need two forms: Heute ist der dritte Oktober. (today is…) and am dritten Oktober (on the third of October). More in our guide to days and months in German.

Clock times in brief

In everyday speech Germans use the 12-hour clock with Viertel (quarter) and halb (half). The trap: halb neun means 8:30 – half to nine, not half past nine. Timetables, shops and the radio use the 24-hour clock.

FAQ: German numbers

Why do Germans say the units before the tens?

It is simply how the language works, a bit like the old English “four and twenty”. 21 is einundzwanzig (one-and-twenty), 57 is siebenundfünfzig (seven-and-fifty). Say the last digit first, then und, then the ten.

Is it eins or ein?

You count eins, zwei, drei. Inside a bigger number and before a noun it becomes ein: einundzwanzig, ein Euro, ein Kaffee. At the end of a number it stays eins: hunderteins (101). For the time you say ein Uhr or es ist eins.

Why do Germans say zwo instead of zwei?

Zwei and drei can sound alike on a bad phone line or over a loudspeaker. Zwo is a clearer alternative for 2 that you will hear in phone numbers, in announcements and whenever people want to be very clear. Both are correct.

How do I write big numbers and decimals in German?

German uses a comma for decimals and a dot (or a space) to group thousands: 3,5 means three point five and 1.000 means one thousand. Numbers below one million are written as one word, for example dreihundertfünfundsechzig.

How do I practise understanding German numbers?

Reading tables is the first step, but numbers are hardest when someone says them fast. The Deutsch30 listening gym has drills for numbers, prices, times, dates and phone numbers, and the vocabulary list for numbers, time and dates helps you review the words.
