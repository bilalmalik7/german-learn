Sie or du? Formal and informal German

German has two words for “you”. Sie (always with a capital S) is polite and formal; du is informal. Choosing the right one matters more in German than in English.

Formal (Sie)Informal (du)English
Wie heißen Sie?Wie heißt du?What's your name?
Woher kommen Sie?Woher kommst du?Where are you from?
Wie geht es Ihnen?Wie geht es dir?How are you?
Können Sie mir helfen?Kannst du mir helfen?Can you help me?
Entschuldigen Sie!Entschuldige!Excuse me!
Auf Wiedersehen!Tschüss!Goodbye! / Bye!
  • Use Sie with strangers, shop staff, officials, doctors, your landlord and – unless told otherwise – at work.
  • Use du with friends, family, children, pets, and usually among young people, students and in online chats.
  • Switching: the older or more senior person normally offers du: Wollen wir uns duzen? (Shall we say du?). The verbs are duzen and siezen.

The full explanation, including the plural ihr, is on the grammar page du, ihr or Sie?

Tips for sounding natural

  • Greet when you enter. In small shops, at the doctor's and in lifts Germans often say Guten Tag or Hallo – and Tschüss or Auf Wiedersehen when they leave.
  • Regional greetings. In southern Germany and Austria you will hear Grüß Gott or Servus; in the north Moin.
  • “Wie geht's?” is a real question among friends. Strangers rarely ask it, and a short honest answer is fine.
  • Ask questions. The question words wer, was, wo, wann, wie, woher unlock most conversations – see all German question words.

FAQ: basic German phrases

What is the most important German phrase for beginners?

Start with Guten Tag, Danke, Bitte and Entschuldigung. Add Ich verstehe das nicht and Können Sie das bitte wiederholen? With these you can stay polite and keep a conversation going even when you do not understand everything.

Should I use Sie or du?

Use Sie with adults you do not know, in shops, at offices and at work, unless people offer du. Use du with friends, family, children and usually among young people and students. If you are not sure, start with Sie.

Is Hallo polite enough?

Hallo is friendly and very common, also with strangers in shops. In formal situations, for example at an office, a job interview or with older people, Guten Tag or Guten Morgen is the safer choice.

What is the difference between Bitte and Danke?

Danke means thank you. Bitte means please, but it is also the answer to Danke (you're welcome) and what you say when you hand something to someone. Wie bitte? means Sorry, what did you say?
