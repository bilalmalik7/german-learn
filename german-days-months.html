Days of the week in German, months and dates

Learn the days of the week in German and the months in German, the four seasons, the little words you need with them (am Montag, im Mai, um acht Uhr), how to say dates like am 3. Oktober – and which holidays Germany celebrates.

Days of the week in German

All seven days are masculine – der Montag, der Dienstag and so on – and like all German nouns they start with a capital letter. On calendars and timetables you will mostly see the two-letter short forms.

EnglishGermanShort
Mondayder MontagMo.
Tuesdayder DienstagDi.
Wednesdayder MittwochMi.
Thursdayder DonnerstagDo.
Fridayder FreitagFr.
Saturdayder Samstag (north/east also: Sonnabend)Sa.
Sundayder SonntagSo.

Two nice details: Mittwoch literally means “mid-week”, and Donnerstag is the day of thunder (Donner). The weekend is das Wochenende – neuter, because the last part, Ende, is neuter. Useful words: heute (today), morgen (tomorrow), gestern (yesterday), übermorgen (the day after tomorrow).

Months in German

The months in German look very similar to English – the challenge is the pronunciation. They are all masculine too: der Januar, der Mai.

EnglishGermanShort
Januaryder JanuarJan.
Februaryder FebruarFeb.
Marchder MärzMärz
Aprilder AprilApr.
Mayder MaiMai
Juneder JuniJuni
Julyder JuliJuli
Augustder AugustAug.
Septemberder SeptemberSept.
Octoberder OktoberOkt.
Novemberder NovemberNov.
Decemberder DezemberDez.

Watch the spelling of März (with ä) and Mai (with ai). In Austria January is usually Jänner. And because Juni and Juli sound alike, many Germans say Juno and Julei when they speak.

am Montag, im Mai, um acht Uhr

English uses on, in and at. German uses am, im and um. am is short for an dem, and im is short for in dem – both are dative.

For …UseExamples
daysamam Montag, am Wochenende
parts of the dayam – but in der Nachtam Morgen, am Abend, in der Nacht
datesamam 3. Oktober (am dritten Oktober)
months and seasonsimim Mai, im Winter
clock timesumum 8 Uhr, um halb neun
yearsno preposition or im Jahr2024 / im Jahr 2024
every weekadd -s, small lettermontags, freitags, sonntags

Combine them from big to small: Am Montag, dem 3. Oktober, um 10 Uhr. Days and parts of the day join into one word: am Montagabend (on Monday evening). More time prepositions such as vor, seit, ab are on the page time prepositions.

Dates: am 3. Oktober

For dates you need ordinal numbers (erste, zweite, dritte…), which you can learn on our German numbers page. In writing, the ordinal gets a dot, and the day comes before the month: 03.10.2026 is 3 October 2026, not 10 March.

  • Welcher Tag ist heute? – Heute ist Montag. (Which day is it today? – Today is Monday.)
  • Der Wievielte ist heute? – Heute ist der dritte Oktober. (What's the date today? – Today is the third of October.)
  • Wann hast du Geburtstag? – Am zwölften Mai. (When is your birthday? – On the twelfth of May.)
  • Der Kurs geht vom 1. bis zum 5. Juni. – read: vom ersten bis zum fünften Juni.

Notice the endings: der dritte after ist, but am dritten after am. The full rules are on the page telling the time, days, months and dates.

German holidays you should know

These are some of the best-known holidays in Germany. Some other public holidays differ from one federal state (Bundesland) to another, so check the local calendar.

HolidayWhenWhat it is
Neujahr1 JanuaryNew Year's Day
Karfreitag & OstermontagMarch or April (date changes)Easter – Ostern
Tag der Arbeit1 MayLabour Day
Tag der Deutschen Einheit3 OctoberGerman Unity Day, the national holiday since 1990
Weihnachten25 and 26 DecemberChristmas – the big family evening is Heiligabend on 24 December
Silvester31 DecemberNew Year's Eve – a big party night, but a normal working day

Seasonal wishes: Frohe Weihnachten! (Merry Christmas!), Frohe Ostern! (Happy Easter!), Guten Rutsch! (said before New Year) and Frohes neues Jahr! (Happy New Year!). Read about a Christmas tradition in Mein erster Weihnachtsmarkt (A2).

FAQ: days, months and dates in German

Are the days and months in German masculine?

Yes. All days of the week, all months and all four seasons are masculine: der Montag, der Mai, der Winter. That is why you say am Montag (an + dem) and im Mai (in + dem).

Do you capitalise days and months in German?

Yes, they are nouns, and all German nouns start with a capital letter: Montag, Januar. The only exception is the adverb form for regular habits, which is written small: montags (on Mondays), abends (in the evenings).

Is it Samstag or Sonnabend?

Both mean Saturday. Samstag is used everywhere and is the safe choice. Sonnabend is common in northern and eastern Germany.

How do I say the date in German?

Use an ordinal number: Heute ist der dritte Oktober (Today is the third of October). For on a date, say am dritten Oktober. In writing, put a dot after the number and the day before the month: am 3. Oktober or 03.10.2026.

Why do Germans say Juno and Julei?

Juni and Juli sound very similar, especially on the phone. To avoid confusion, many people say Juno for June and Julei for July in spoken German. In writing, always use Juni and Juli.
